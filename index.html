Generative AI Driven Simulation for
Virtual Testing of Autonomous Vehicles



Overview


The organizers are pleased to propose a tutorial on Generative AI–Driven Simulation for Autonomous Vehicle Testing, to be hosted at the IEEE Intelligent Vehicles Symposium 2026. This tutorial will introduce TeraSim, a generative-AI–driven platform that automatically constructs city-scale digital twins for scalable, safety-critical testing of autonomous vehicles (AVs) before physical deployment.

Unlike conventional on-road testing and replay-based simulators that are costly, unsafe, and limited in diversity, TeraSim leverages foundation models and large-scale data to generate realistic, continuously evolving virtual environments. The tutorial will feature invited talks from both academia and industry, highlighting TeraSim's core components, including: 1) cityNDE, which learns long-term, naturalistic city-scale driving behaviors; 2) Adversity orchestrator, which synthesizes rare but safety-critical scenarios guided by real-world crash data and multi-modal large language models; 3) Sensor simulation like Cosmos-Drive, a world foundation model enabling photorealistic multi-view driving video generation. Use cases from academic and industrial partners (e.g., Bot Auto, Parallel Domain) will also be presented.

Participants will gain a deep understanding of how generative AI and foundation models are reshaping AV simulation—from static, graphics-based engines to intelligent, self-evolving digital twins. The tutorial will benefit researchers, engineers, and policymakers seeking scalable tools for safety validation, bridging the sim-to-real gap and accelerating trustworthy autonomous mobility.


Keywords

  • Methodological frameworks for safety testing and validation
  • World models and video foundation models for AVs
  • Large language models for scenario generation
  • Naturalistic driving environment modeling with statistical realism
  • Digital twins for virtual testing of autonomous vehicles
  • Adversarial testing and validation frameworks, adversarial attacks, and falsification of AV sub-modules
  • Trustworthy AI to enhance the safety for AVs
  • Real-time SOTIF risk monitoring for AVs
  • Field implementation, simulation, small-scale testbeds, and multi-fidelity (sim-to-real) frameworks
  • Formal methods and runtime verification of AV
